## 1. Foundational reference point: what SonoNet actually did (and didn't) do

Baumgartner et al., *SonoNet: Real-Time Detection and Localisation of Fetal Standard Scan Planes in Freehand Ultrasound*, IEEE TMI 2017 ([arXiv:1612.05601](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05601), [PMC6051487](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6051487/)).

Key facts, confirmed directly from the paper text:

- **Task framing is fundamentally different from ours.** SonoNet classifies 13 standard views **plus background/non-standard** from continuous **freehand video of full clinical anomaly-screening examinations** — i.e., the overwhelming majority of frames in its evaluation videos are *not* standard planes, and the system must find the needles in that haystack in real time. It reports **F1 = 0.798** in this "realistic classification experiment modelling real-time detection," and **90.09%** accuracy for *retrospective frame retrieval* (pulling the single best frame for a given view out of a saved video) — these are two distinct, both video-native evaluation protocols.
- **Weakly-supervised localisation is the headline technical contribution, not classification accuracy.** SonoNet is a VGG-style CNN trained with *image-level labels only*; a backward pass through the same trained network produces a **class-conditional saliency/CAM-style bounding box** at inference time, with **77.8% localisation accuracy**, *without ever training on a single bounding box*. This is the same family of technique as Grad-CAM (both derive from CAM/backprop saliency), but SonoNet's paper treats it as a first-class deliverable — a box drawn every frame, sized to plausibly contain the anatomy — not merely a debug/interpretability overlay.
- Real-time performance was framed around the actual scanning hardware constraint at the time; the reported accuracy is measured on full clinical video, not on a curated, class-balanced static-image benchmark.

**Direct comparison to our system, from what's in the project files:**

| Dimension | SonoNet (2017) | Our system (per `EVAL_REPORT.md`, `EXPERIMENTS.md`, phase docs) |
|---|---|---|
| Training/eval data | Freehand video of real clinical exams, video-native metrics (F1 on video, retrospective retrieval) | FETAL_PLANES_DB static curated images (12,400, patient-disjoint); no freehand video was ever used for classifier *training or accuracy evaluation* — video is used only for temporal-smoothing tuning (Phase 5) and stability metrics (Phase 6), not for measuring plane-classification accuracy itself |
| Classes | 13 standard views + background, learned jointly | 7 planes + "Other" (8-way single softmax) — comparable in spirit but our "Other" was never validated against a realistic prevalence of non-standard frames in continuous video the way SonoNet's was |
| Localisation | Weakly-supervised bounding box from the classifier itself, 77.8% accuracy, delivered as a real feature | Grad-CAM overlay only, explicitly framed as a debug/explanation aid, never converted into a box; genuine box output was attempted separately via a fully-supervised RetinaNet multitask head that was trained for **1 epoch only** (val macro-F1 0.8421, below the production classifier) and is explicitly **not used** in Demo 1 (`docs/phases/demo1/demo1_walkthrough.md` §7.1) |
| Test macro-F1 (curated in-distribution) | Not the framing used | 0.8927 (`convnext_tiny.fb_in22k_ft_in1k`, `EVAL_REPORT.md` §1) |
| Cross-device/cross-site generalization | Reported degradation is implicit in the freehand/video framing itself | 83.2% combined HC18+UCL vs. 98.0% in-distribution collapsed baseline, a −14.8pp gap on Head (`EVAL_REPORT.md` §2) — this is a genuinely comparable "real-world generalization" number, and it is honestly reported |
| Real video plane-transition validation | Implicit — video *is* the primary evaluation medium | Historically absent (Phase 5/6 explicitly documented "zero real transitions available to time"); per your latest update, now addressed with **8 new synthetic multiplane clips with deliberate transitions and frame-level annotations**, giving 39 annotated transitions, mean latency-to-stable 489.3 ms, median 500 ms, P90 525 ms |

**Verdict on "is SonoNet superior to us":** Not straightforwardly — the two systems optimize for different things. SonoNet is stronger on: (a) genuinely video-native accuracy claims, (b) weakly-supervised localisation delivered as a real, always-on feature at zero extra annotation cost, (c) broader view coverage (13 views incl. cardiac views) learned end-to-end. Our system is stronger on: (a) modern backbone/training rigor (patient-disjoint splits, bootstrap significance testing across 6 backbones, documented negative ablations), (b) a considerably more sophisticated and *empirically tuned* temporal-stability layer (two-stage EMA+hysteresis+dwell, then majority-vote filter, both parameter-swept against real/synthetic video with an explicit no-spurious-switch gate) — SonoNet's 2017 paper has no analogue to this at all, (c) an actual shipped, threaded, real-time pipeline with instrumented FPS/latency and a usable web UI, none of which SonoNet's paper describes building. **The single most consequential, directly-portable idea from SonoNet that we have not adopted is #2 — free bounding-box localisation via weak supervision from the classifier we already have**, discussed in detail in §7 below.

## 2. The line of work SonoNet spawned, and where it's gone since (2018–2026)

### 2.1 Video-based, temporal, and predictive standard-plane methods

- **ScanAhead** (Men, Zhao, Drukker, Papageorghiou, Noble, *Medical Image Analysis* 104, Aug 2025, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841525001616), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40446481/)) reframes the problem entirely: instead of classifying/smoothing per-frame predictions, it **predicts/synthesizes the target standard-plane image itself** from a short "search clip" of video *approaching* that plane, using a Swin-Transformer spatial autoencoder + TCN temporal extractor + a domain-offset adaptor, plus a predicted probe-rotation signal to guide the sonographer toward the standard plane. This is a genuinely different and more ambitious framing than temporal smoothing — it's proactive guidance, not reactive stabilization. It is **not a drop-in replacement** for our Tier-1/Tier-2 stack (different objective, needs paired search-clip→SP-image training data we don't have), but it is the clearest evidence in the current literature that "video methods for fetal planes" have moved past simple smoothing toward *forecasting*. This is a legitimate "future research direction," not a near-term implementation item.
- **TIER-LOC** (Mishra, Saha, Zhao, Hernandez-Cruz, Patey, Papageorghiou, Noble, *MedIA* 2025) — multi-tier transformer for **visual-query video clip localisation** in fetal ultrasound video (finding *where in a long recording* a given anatomical view/event occurs). Adjacent to our problem but solves clip retrieval, not per-frame classification; relevant mainly as an indicator that transformer-based video search over fetal ultrasound is an active MedIA-track research area, worth knowing about but not directly implementable here.
- **Memory-based unsupervised video clinical quality assessment** (Zhao, Zheng, Teng, Yasrab, Drukker, Papageorghiou, Noble, *MedIA* 90, 2023, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841523002372); earlier MICCAI 2022 version) — trains a spatio-temporal memory-based bidirectional reconstruction model on **high-quality video only**, with no anatomical annotations, and flags low-quality video via reconstruction error; augmented with optical flow and sonographer gaze as auxiliary modalities. AUC 0.911 on HC/cerebellar-diameter measurement quality. This is squarely in the "quality assessment / clinically acceptable standard plane" bucket you specifically asked about, and it is a **video-level**, not single-frame, method — genuinely complementary to (not competing with) our per-frame classifier.

### 2.2 Fetal-plane classification architecture literature since Burgos-Artizzu (2020)

Systematic review: *Uncertainty-Calibrated Explainable AI for Fetal Ultrasound Plane Classification: A Systematic Review* ([arXiv:2601.00990](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00990)) — this is the **same paper already cited in your own `PHASE_4_KICKOFF_PROMPT.md` §9** (median SSL-pretraining gain of ~3.1pp, IQR 1.8–4.9), so your prior literature review did find and correctly use it. Confirmed from it directly: across a pooled meta-analysis of studies on 6-class fetal-plane classification, ResNet-50/101 remains the single most common backbone (32 studies), followed by EfficientNet (14) and ViT variants (12) and hybrid CNN-Transformer designs (7); only 5 studies used self-supervised ultrasound-domain foundation models, and only 3 used federated multi-centre training.

Newer architecture-specific papers found that are **not** in your existing `EXPERIMENTS.md`:

- **COMFormer** (Sarker, Rai, Karnawat, Tiwari, Singh, Roy, Alsharid, Papageorghiou, Noble, *IEEE Trans. Ultrasonics, Ferroelectrics, and Freq. Control* 70(11), 2023, [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10239175/)) — a **two-stage cascade** (exactly the design pattern your project's own `00_PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` §4 explicitly rejected in favor of one 8-way softmax) using a residual cross-covariance-attention (XCA) transformer: Stage 1 classifies the 6 coarse planes, and **a separate model is trained specifically on the 4-way brain subtype problem** (trans-thalamic / trans-cerebellum / trans-ventricular / non-brain), reporting 96.33% accuracy on that isolated brain-only split. This is directly relevant to your single, persistent weak spot: `Brain_Trans_ventricular` (F1 0.77, and your own focal-loss ablation *regressed* it further, per `EXPERIMENTS.md`). COMFormer's result is not apples-to-apples with yours (different split, different metric — accuracy on an isolated 4-way problem vs. your F1 inside an 8-way patient-disjoint task, and their maternal-fetal split is 95.64% "accuracy" on what looks like an easier stratification than our patient-disjoint FETAL_PLANES_DB test set) — but the *design principle* (give the brain sub-planes their own dedicated classification stage/loss/attention rather than diluting the gradient across 8 classes with one shared softmax) is exactly the kind of targeted intervention your own bug-audit culture would sanction, and it's a plausible answer to the finding in your confusion-matrix analysis that ~25/27 of `Brain_Trans_ventricular`'s off-diagonal errors land on `Brain_Trans_thalamic` specifically.
- **Generative Adversarial Networks to Improve Fetal Brain Fine-Grained Plane Classification** (Montero, Bonet-Carne, Burgos-Artizzu — the *same author* as your own FETAL_PLANES_DB dataset — *Sensors* 21(23), 2021, [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/23/7975), [PMC8659720](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8659720/)). Trains separate StyleGAN2-ADA generators for trans-thalamic and trans-ventricular images specifically to generate additional synthetic training examples for **exactly the confusion pair your model still gets wrong**, on the same dataset family you use. This is a directly transferable, moderate-effort idea: a class-conditional GAN (or even a simpler diffusion-based augmenter) trained only on the ~600–1,600 brain-subplane images you already have, used purely as a targeted oversampling mechanism for the minority/hard classes — orthogonal to (and stackable with) your existing inverse-frequency class weighting.
- **Adaptive Contrast Adjustment Module (ACAM)** ([arXiv:2509.00808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00808)) — a lightweight, plug-and-play preprocessing sub-network (predicts K clinically-plausible contrast transforms, fuses the resulting multi-contrast views before the backbone) validated on the **same 12,400-image, six-category FETAL_PLANES_DB dataset you use**. Reported gains: **+2.02pp accuracy on lightweight models, +1.29pp on "conventional" models, +1.15pp on state-of-the-art models** (their own EfficientNet/ConvNeXt-class baselines). Because this is architecture-agnostic and sits only in the low layers, it is one of the cheapest, most directly portable ideas in this whole survey — a few hours of implementation against your existing `src/data/transforms.py` pipeline, no retraining infrastructure changes, testable as a clean ablation against your locked `convnext_tiny` baseline.
- **Krishna & Kokil**, stacked ensemble of deep learning models (*Expert Systems with Applications* 238, 2024) and multi-layer-perceptron deep-feature-integration variants — incremental ensembling work on the same public dataset; useful only as further evidence that ensembling still buys a small amount of accuracy on this task, but not something we'd recommend given your project's already-strong single-model bootstrap-validated result and your explicit single-checkpoint deployment target.
- **"Novel neural network classification... through optimized feature selection"** (Rathika et al., *BMC Medical Imaging* 2024) and the **graph-enhanced multi-scale ensemble** (Gao et al., *Eng. Appl. AI* 2024) — feature-selection/ensembling papers on the same public dataset; marginal, not recommended for adoption given cost/benefit.

### 2.3 Ultrasound-domain foundation models / self-supervised pretraining — a real update to your own prior finding

This is the area where your original literature review (documented in `PHASE_4_KICKOFF_PROMPT.md` §9) is now **measurably out of date**, through no fault of the earlier work — several of these models postdate your Phase 4 research window:

- **FetalCLIP** (Maani et al., BioMedIA-MBZUAI, [arXiv:2502.14807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14807), [GitHub](https://github.com/biomedia-mbzuai/fetalclip)) — a CLIP-style vision-language model pretrained on **210,035 fetal ultrasound images paired with GPT-4o-generated captions**, the largest paired fetal-US dataset used for foundation-model training to date. Demonstrates strong zero-shot standard-view classification and, notably, **works well from very little labeled data** (comparable accuracy to a competitor trained on the full dataset using only 32 patients' worth of fine-tuning data).
- **USFM** (Jiao et al., 2024) — ViT-based, masked-image-modeling SSL on ~2.19M general ultrasound images across organs (not fetal-specific), reports 93.6% accuracy on a fetal-plane classification benchmark, beating a from-scratch ViT and matching/edging out ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet121.
- A dedicated, rigorous **benchmark paper published specifically to answer "which foundation model should we use for fetal plane classification"**: *Benchmarking Ultrasound Foundation Models for Fetal Plane Classification* ([arXiv:2605.27796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27796)). Findings, quoted directly: under **linear probing** (frozen encoder), **FetalCLIP wins** (F1 0.9261 in-domain / 0.9731 out-of-domain on a held-out African cohort); under **full fine-tuning**, **USFM wins** (F1 0.9476 in-domain / 0.9515 out-of-domain); **MOFO and UltraSAM (both segmentation-oriented ViT foundation models) underperform even plain ImageNet-pretrained CNN/ViT baselines** — a genuinely useful negative result, since it shows foundation-model provenance matters more than parameter count or training-set size, and a wrong choice can actively hurt you.

**Why this matters for your project specifically:** your Phase 4 pretraining-init ablation correctly concluded "FUSC checkpoint not portable — ResNet encoder vs. our ConvNeXt/RepVGG/EfficientNet family — skip, don't force it" (`EXPERIMENTS.md`). That reasoning is sound but is now **stale as a final answer**, because FetalCLIP and USFM did not exist (or weren't discoverable) at the time of that ablation, and — critically — neither is a drop-in replacement for `convnext_tiny.fb_in22k_ft_in1k` either (FetalCLIP's image encoder is a CLIP ViT, USFM's is also ViT-based with MIM pretraining), so the *same* architecture-mismatch objection that killed FUSC applies again at the weight-swap level. What's different this time is that both papers, and the independent benchmark paper, report results **on classification tasks specifically, with fine-tuning recipes documented**, so revisiting this ablation is now feasible in a way it wasn't with FUSC. This is a genuine, evidence-backed reason to re-open a question your project closed for the right reasons at the time.

### 2.4 Image-quality / "clinically acceptable standard plane" assessment — a real gap in scope, not just execution

This is the most clinically substantive category the survey turned up, and it maps directly onto something your project has never attempted:

- **Quality Assessment of Fetal Head Ultrasound via Faster R-CNN** (Yaqub-style constraint-checking lineage; [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-01045-4_5)) — detects the presence/visibility of the specific anatomical landmarks (cavum septum pellucidi, thalami, etc.) that define a *clinically acceptable* trans-thalamic plane and scores the image by how many are present, rather than just asking "which of 8 classes is this."
- **A Deep Learning Solution for Automatic Fetal Neurosonographic Diagnostic Plane Verification Using Clinical Standard Constraints** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301562917303320)) — explicitly checks head magnification, brain symmetry, and CSP visibility against the Salomon et al. clinical criteria, on 19,838 images from 10,595 routine scans across multiple machines.
- **Effectiveness and clinical impact of AI-based first-trimester image quality auditing** ([BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11956207/)) — a YOLOv7 structure-detector + regression network scoring image quality 0–100 on the four key first-trimester planes, **validated with a prospective before/after clinical audit across two radiologist panels**, showing measurable improvement in the proportion of standard-quality images obtained after AI feedback was introduced.
- **AI-based quality control in low-resource settings — West China real-world multicenter study** ([BMC Medicine, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-026-04768-1)) — 34-hospital deployment of an AI quality-control system with a 0–100 quality score and standard/basic-standard/non-standard tiers, run prospectively since 2020.

**The gap this exposes:** your system answers *"what anatomical plane is this frame"* and, via smoothing, *"how stable is that answer over time."* It never answers *"is this a diagnostically usable rendition of that plane"* — i.e., a frame that is correctly classified as `Fetal_femur` but is blurry, poorly angled, or has the femur only partially in view would score exactly the same as a textbook-perfect femur frame. Your own `07_STRETCH_GOALS_AND_ROADMAP.md` Stretch Goal 1 (multi-task detection head) gestures at this by wanting structure-level boxes, but the specific "constraint satisfaction against a clinical checklist" framing used by the papers above is a different, complementary, and arguably more clinically load-bearing idea than bounding boxes alone.

### 2.5 Attention/transformer architectures more broadly

- **Hybrid State-Space + ViT framework** ([*Diagnostics* 15(22), 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics15222879)) — combines an SSM (Mamba-family) with a ViT for sequential + global reasoning, multi-task learning branch. Interesting but unvalidated against a patient-disjoint, bootstrap-tested baseline the way your project already is; treat as (c) future-research signal only.
- **Light-Weight CNN Attention-Based architectures** ([arXiv:2410.17396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.17396)) — squeeze-excitation-style attention bolted onto EfficientNet/MobileNet-class backbones, reports 96.25% top-1 on the standard FETAL_PLANES_DB split; broadly consistent with (not exceeding) your own EfficientNet-Lite0/MobileNetV3 numbers once you account for different train/test protocols — a caution against overclaiming from this line of work (see the apples-to-oranges warning in §6).

## 6. A caution on apples-to-oranges comparisons (addressing your explicit concern)

Several numbers surfaced in this survey look superficially better than your 0.8927 test macro-F1 (e.g., 95–96% "accuracy" figures from COMFormer, the light-weight-attention paper, USFM's 93.6% fetal-plane accuracy). None of these are safely comparable to your number without qualification, because:

1. **Metric mismatch.** Most of these papers report top-1 **accuracy** on a class-imbalanced 6-class or 4-class problem (where "Other"/majority classes dominate), while your headline number is **macro-F1** on an 8-class problem — a metric you deliberately chose specifically because accuracy lets majority classes mask minority-class failure (per your own `04_MODEL_TRAINING.md` §3 rationale, which is the methodologically stronger choice, not a weaker one).
2. **Split provenance.** Very few of the surveyed papers state whether their split is patient-disjoint. Your project enforces and *verifies* patient-level disjointness with a hard assertion (`verify_no_leakage.py`) — a discipline that is explicitly *not* universal in this literature (indeed, this is precisely the failure mode your own `02_DATASETS.md` flagged for the `FP`/`MULTICENTRE` bundle). A paper reporting 96% on an unverified or image-level split is not directly comparable to your patient-disjoint 89.27%, and may in fact be *inflated* relative to a genuinely held-out number.
3. **Statistical significance.** Almost none of the surveyed papers report anything like your bootstrap confidence-interval analysis (`docs/phases/phase_04/bootstrap_significance_output.txt`) distinguishing "robust win" from "statistically indistinguishable." This is a genuine methodological strength on your side that is rare in the surveyed literature, not a gap.

The practical implication: don't chase any of these headline numbers directly. Where a paper's *method* (not its number) is transferable and independently well-motivated (ACAM, targeted GAN augmentation, foundation-model linear probing), evaluate it against your own locked test set and bootstrap protocol, exactly as you did for the RepVGG/EfficientNetV2/ConvNeXt comparison and the focal-loss ablation.

## 7. What is genuinely, concretely actionable — the weakly-supervised localisation opportunity in detail

Because you specifically asked to study SonoNet's localisation approach and determine whether it's applicable, here is the concrete mechanism and why it matters for your stalled Stretch Goal 1:

SonoNet's localisation is not a separate detection network — it is a **backward pass through the already-trained classification network**, using the gradient/activation of the predicted class with respect to a late convolutional feature map, thresholded and bounding-boxed. This is architecturally the same family as Grad-CAM, which **you have already fully implemented, verified per-backbone (`src/models/gradcam.py`), and wired into both the live pipeline and the offline renderer**. The only missing piece between what you have and a SonoNet-style "free" bounding box is: (1) thresholding the CAM heatmap (e.g., Otsu or a fixed percentile) into a binary mask, and (2) taking the mask's bounding rectangle. This requires **zero new training, zero new annotations, and no dependency on the stalled 1-epoch multitask/RetinaNet model** that Demo 1 currently cannot use for exactly this purpose (`docs/phases/demo1/demo1_walkthrough.md` §7.1: "detection head produces garbage bounding boxes").

This is squarely a category-(a) "directly applicable, worth implementing now" item: it would let Demo 1 display a plausible, weakly-supervised anatomy box **today**, using the classifier you already trust, entirely sidestepping the HC18/UCL bounding-box-annotation pipeline whose leakage/patient-ID bugs consumed significant engineering effort in Phase 7 (`docs/phases/phase_07/goal_1_walkthrough.md`, 14 documented bugs). It would not be as accurate as SonoNet's purpose-built weak-localisation training (which optimizes the network partly *for* localisation quality, not just classification), so it should be framed to users as an approximate saliency-derived region, not a validated measurement — but that framing is honest and no weaker than what your Grad-CAM overlay already implicitly claims.

## 8. Prioritized recommendations

### (1) What we are currently missing
- A **quality-assessment / clinical-constraint layer** distinguishing "correct class" from "diagnostically acceptable rendition of that class" — entirely absent from the current scope (§2.4).
- **Real, freehand video** with genuine ground-truth transitions and non-plane segments (NatalIA) — your synthetic multiplane clips are a good stopgap but not a substitute, and your own project's checklist already flagged NatalIA as the intended fix, unactioned.
- A **targeted intervention for the `Brain_Trans_ventricular`/`Brain_Trans_thalamic` confusion** beyond class weighting and the (negative-result) focal-loss ablation — both a cascade/dedicated-head design (COMFormer) and GAN-based targeted augmentation (Montero et al.) exist in the literature and neither has been tried.
- A **re-examination of domain-specific pretraining**, now that FetalCLIP and USFM exist and have been benchmarked specifically on fetal-plane classification (unlike FUSC, which correctly justified skipping this in Phase 4).
- **Free weakly-supervised localisation from the existing Grad-CAM pipeline**, unlocking a functioning "show me the anatomy" feature in Demo 1 without waiting on the stalled multitask detection line.

### (2) What should be incorporated into the existing project (highest ROI, lowest cost, ordered)
1. **CAM-to-bounding-box localisation from the existing Grad-CAM output** (§7). Near-zero engineering cost, reuses code you already trust, unblocks a real Demo-1-visible feature today.
2. **ACAM-style adaptive-contrast preprocessing module** (§2.2) as an ablation against the locked `convnext_tiny` checkpoint — cheap, architecture-agnostic, reported consistent (if modest) gains on the identical dataset you use.
3. **NatalIA PBF-US1 download and integration** as the real-video transition-latency validation set your own roadmap already called for — this is the one action that would let you replace "measured on synthetic multiplane clips" with "measured on real free-hand sweep video," which is the single most credible upgrade available to your transition-latency claim.
4. **A targeted ablation on the brain-subplane confusion**: either (a) a small StyleGAN2-ADA or diffusion-based augmenter trained only on the ~600–1,600 brain-subplane images to oversample the hard pair (Montero et al.), or (b) a lightweight second classification head/loss term specifically for the 4-way brain-subtype problem in the spirit of COMFormer, evaluated with your existing bootstrap-CI protocol against the current `convnext_tiny` baseline before deciding whether it's a real win.

### (3) What should not be changed
- The **single 8-way softmax over the two-stage cascade** as the primary architecture — COMFormer's evidence for cascades is suggestive for the hard brain classes specifically, not a case for reversing your whole-system design; keep the single softmax and treat the brain-subplane fix (if pursued) as a local intervention, not a rearchitecture.
- **Backbone choice and selection methodology** (ConvNeXt-Tiny via 6-way bootstrap-validated comparison) — nothing surveyed here beats it on a comparable, patient-disjoint, statistically-tested basis; the literature's higher-looking numbers are not safely comparable (§6).
- **The Tier-1 + Tier-2a temporal-smoothing design.** No paper surveyed proposes anything simpler and better-validated for this exact sub-problem (residual periodic oscillation surviving EMA+hysteresis+dwell); ScanAhead solves an adjacent but different problem (predictive plane synthesis) at a much higher implementation cost and data requirement that isn't justified by current evidence of insufficiency in your own system.
- **The FP/MULTICENTRE exclusion and patient-disjoint splitting discipline** — independently corroborated as correct by the source paper itself; don't relax it for any perceived accuracy gain from an unverified-split competitor's number.
- **The explicit non-goal of clinical/regulatory validation for v1** — correct scoping given the project's stated purpose; the field's emerging prospective-validation bar (§4) is real but appropriately deferred, not neglected.

### (4) Worthwhile as a next research phase (not v1)
- **ScanAhead-style predictive plane guidance** (forecasting the standard plane from an approaching search clip, plus probe-motion guidance) — a materially different, harder problem requiring paired search-clip→standard-plane training data you don't have; genuinely interesting, genuinely expensive.
- **Video-level unsupervised quality assessment** (Zhao et al.'s memory-based reconstruction approach) as a second signal alongside your existing per-frame classifier and smoothing stack.
- **VLM/foundation-model zero-shot and few-shot exploration** (FetalCLIP zero-shot classification, natural-language explanation generation) — an emerging 2025–2026 direction (also see FetalMind, "visually grounded language model for fetal ultrasound understanding") that is promising but immature relative to your current supervised pipeline's rigor.
- **True mobile/edge deployment** (ONNX/TensorRT export + INT8 quantization + optional knowledge distillation) — already correctly scoped as Stretch Goal 3 in your own roadmap; revisit once/if a real deployment target beyond the RTX 4060 demo exists.

### (5) SonoNet and successors vs. our system — summary verdict
Neither system is strictly superior. SonoNet (2017) is stronger on video-native evaluation realism and on delivering weakly-supervised localisation as a real, zero-extra-cost feature — a specific, well-understood technique you have the pieces to adopt cheaply (§7) and currently do not. Its 2023–2025 successors (COMFormer, ScanAhead, the video quality-assessment line) each push further into either fine-grained brain-subplane handling, predictive/generative video guidance, or unsupervised quality assessment — none of which your system currently attempts, and each of which represents a genuinely different research bet rather than a strict improvement on what you've built. Your system is stronger on backbone/training rigor, patient-disjoint data discipline, statistically validated backbone selection, the sophistication and empirical tuning of its temporal-stability layer (which has no real analogue in the SonoNet lineage), and shipped, instrumented, real-time engineering. The honest framing is: **SonoNet solved a harder, more clinically realistic version of the *detection* problem than you have attempted, using a decade-old but still-relevant technique (weak localisation) you haven't adopted; you have solved a harder version of the *stability/serving* problem than SonoNet's lineage generally attempts.**

### (6) Does this prevent calling v1 "technically mature"?
No single gap found here is disqualifying for a demo/portfolio-scoped v1, and none contradicts your project's own stated non-goals. The two gaps worth taking seriously before calling the *evaluation story* (not the engineering) fully mature are: (a) the absence of any quality/clinical-acceptability signal beyond plane identity (§2.4), and (b) the fact that your transition-latency number, however now measured and persisted correctly, still rests on **synthetic** rather than **real** transitions (§3, NatalIA). Both are honestly disclosable limitations rather than defects, and both already have a clear, moderate-cost path to closure using ideas surfaced in this survey.
